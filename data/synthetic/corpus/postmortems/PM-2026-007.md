# PM-2026-007: Stale prices shown after price update  
2026-06-16 | SEV3 | web-storefront  

## Summary  
A CDN caching rule unintentionally cached rendered product pages for up to 4 hours. When a price update was published, customers continued to see the old price on product pages for up to 3.5 hours, although the checkout process applied the correct price. The issue was identified and resolved within 210 minutes.

## Impact  
- Product pages displayed stale prices for as long as 3.5 hours after the price change.  
- Checkout transactions charged the correct, updated price, so no financial loss occurred.  
- Customer support received tickets reporting price mismatches.

## Detection  
The problem was first noticed through customer support tickets submitted around 09:30 UTC.

## Timeline  
- **08:00** – Price update published.  
- **09:30** – Support tickets opened reporting price mismatches.  
- **11:10** – The misbehaving CDN rule was identified.  
- **11:30** – CDN cache purge completed and rule scoped correctly.

## Root cause  
A CDN rule intended for static assets also matched the `/p/*` product page path. Consequently, rendered HTML containing price information was cached at edge locations.

## Trigger  
The CDN rule’s overly broad path pattern caused it to cache dynamic product pages, which should have been excluded.

## Resolution  
- The CDN rule was narrowed to apply only to `/static` paths.  
- The cached content was purged from the CDN edge nodes.

## Contributing factors  
None reported.

## Action items  
- Ensure all CDN rule changes are reviewed by the Storefront team (completed).

## Lessons learned  
- CDN caching rules must be scoped precisely to avoid unintentionally caching dynamic content.  
- A review process for CDN configuration changes helps catch overly broad patterns before deployment.  
- Monitoring price update rollouts and verifying front‑end rendering can provide early detection of caching anomalies.
