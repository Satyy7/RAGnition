# PM-2026-009: Signed-in customers rejected at checkout after key rotation  
**Date:** 2026-08-12 **Severity:** SEV2 **Service:** auth‑service  

## Summary  
A quarterly manual rotation of the auth‑service token signing key removed the previous public key from the JWKS endpoint before all downstream consumers had refreshed their cached key sets. Consumers that still held the old key attempted to verify tokens signed with the new key, resulting in “kid not found” errors and 401 responses for signed‑in checkout requests. The issue persisted for 95 minutes until both keys were republished and consumer pods were restarted, flushing their caches.

## Impact  
- Approximately 30 % of signed‑in checkout requests returned HTTP 401.  
- The outage lasted 95 minutes.  
- Customers were prompted to sign in again, generating support tickets.

## Detection  
- The `CheckoutAuthFailures` alert fired at 06:07.  
- Customer support received tickets reporting repeated sign‑in prompts during checkout.

## Timeline  
- **06:00** – Rotation script runs, publishing the new signing key and immediately removing the previous public key from the JWKS endpoint.  
- **06:07** – `CheckoutAuthFailures` alert triggers.  
- **06:50** – Checkout‑api logs contain “kid not found in JWKS”.  
- **07:35** – Both old and new keys are republished to the JWKS endpoint; checkout‑api pods are restarted, causing consumer JWKS caches to be flushed and verification to succeed.  

## Root cause  
The rotation script removed the previous public key from the JWKS endpoint instantly. Downstream consumers cached the JWKS for up to 24 hours, so they continued to use the old key set and could not verify tokens signed with the newly rotated key, resulting in “kid not found” verification failures.

## Trigger  
Quarterly manual rotation of the auth‑service token signing key.

## Resolution  
- Republished both the old and new signing keys to the JWKS endpoint.  
- Restarted checkout‑api pods, which forced consumers to refetch the JWKS and cleared their caches.

## Contributing factors  
- The JWKS cache duration in consumer services was not documented in the auth‑service repository, leading to an assumption that key removal could be immediate.

## Action items  
- **Key publishing policy:** Keep the previous signing key published for at least the longest consumer JWKS cache time (24 h) after a rotation. This has been added to the rotation runbook.  
- **Consumer behavior:** Implement logic for consumers to refetch the JWKS when they encounter an unknown `kid`. Issue opened as CHK‑410.

## Lessons learned  
- **Grace period for key rotation:** Removing a signing key from the JWKS endpoint must be delayed until all known consumers have had sufficient time to expire their caches.  
- **Documentation of cache behavior:** Explicitly documenting JWKS cache durations in auth‑service guides prevents mismatched expectations during key rotations.  
- **Defensive consumer design:** Consumers should handle unknown `kid` values gracefully by triggering an immediate JWKS refresh rather than relying solely on cache expiration.
