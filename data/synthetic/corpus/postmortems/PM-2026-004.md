# PM-2026-004: Order confirmation emails delayed during marketing campaign  
Date: 2026-02-27 | Severity: SEV3 | Service: notification-service  

## Summary  
During a marketing campaign on 27 Feb 2026, order confirmation emails sent by the notification‑service were delayed by up to 2.5 hours. The delay was caused by the shared PostRelay rate‑limit being exhausted, leading to 429 responses and immediate retry loops that kept the account throttled.

## Impact  
Customers received order confirmation emails up to 2.5 hours later than expected, potentially affecting the perceived reliability of the checkout experience.

## Detection  
The incident was first detected by the **EmailQueueBacklog** alert, which fired at 15:20 when the email queue length exceeded the configured threshold.

## Timeline  
- **15:00** – Marketing campaign starts, consuming most of the shared PostRelay limit of 100 messages/s.  
- **15:20** – EmailQueueBacklog alert fires, indicating a growing backlog of transactional emails.  
- **16:10** – Logs show repeated 429 responses from PostRelay and immediate retry loops without backoff.  
- **17:40** – Campaign is paused; the backlog drains and email delivery returns to normal.  

## Root cause  
PostRelay returned HTTP 429 (Too Many Requests). The notification‑service retried each failed request immediately, without exponential backoff or respecting the `Retry-After` header. The rapid retries consumed the remaining quota, keeping the account in a throttled state and preventing timely delivery of transactional emails.

## Trigger  
A marketing campaign that used the majority of the shared PostRelay rate‑limit (100 messages/s) left insufficient capacity for the notification‑service’s transactional email traffic.

## Resolution  
The marketing campaign was paused, allowing the PostRelay quota to recover. Once the quota was restored, the email backlog drained by 17:40, and normal email processing resumed.

## Contributing factors  
- **Shared rate limit** between marketing and transactional email traffic on a single PostRelay account.  
- **Retry logic** in the notification‑service lacked exponential backoff, jitter, and handling of the `Retry-After` header, causing aggressive retry loops.

## Action items  
- Implement retries with exponential backoff, jitter, and respect for `Retry-After` (completed, CHG-2215).  
- Request a separate PostRelay sub‑account dedicated to transactional email (open).

## Lessons learned  
- Isolating rate‑limited resources for different traffic classes (marketing vs. transactional) prevents one workload from starving another.  
- Retry mechanisms must incorporate backoff and respect server‑provided throttling signals to avoid exacerbating rate‑limit violations.  
- Monitoring alerts should be coupled with runbooks that include immediate throttling mitigation steps, such as pausing non‑critical traffic.
