# PM-2026-002: Customers lost cart contents during January sale  
**Date:** 2026-01-19 **Severity:** SEV2 **Service:** checkout-api  

## Summary  
During the January sale, the checkout‑api’s cart cache (Redis) reached its configured memory limit. Because the Redis instance used the `allkeys-lru` eviction policy, active cart keys were evicted alongside stale data, resulting in loss of cart contents for a subset of customers.

## Impact  
- Approximately 6 % of active carts disappeared.  
- Affected customers were forced to rebuild their carts, leading to a degraded shopping experience and increased support workload.

## Detection  
- Initial indication came from customer support tickets filed at 11:40 AM.  
- The issue was corroborated by a spike in the **CartRecreateRate** dashboard, which tracks the rate of cart recreations.

## Timeline  
- **11:00 AM** – Sale begins; traffic spikes.  
- **11:40 AM** – First support tickets received reporting missing carts.  
- **12:30 PM** – Monitoring showed the `evicted_keys` counter climbing rapidly.  
- **13:00 PM** – Memory limit increased from 2 GB to 4 GB and eviction policy changed.

## Root cause  
The Redis instance backing the cart cache was configured with `maxmemory-policy allkeys-lru`. When the cache hit its `maxmemory` limit, Redis evicted keys indiscriminately, including active cart entries.

## Trigger  
High sale traffic filled the cart cache to its `maxmemory` threshold, activating the eviction mechanism.

## Resolution  
- Increased Redis `maxmemory` from 2 GB to 4 GB.  
- Switched eviction policy to `volatile-lru` and ensured all cart keys have a TTL (implemented in change CHG‑2201).

## Contributing factors  
- Mixed long‑lived and disposable data were stored in the same Redis instance without a consistent TTL discipline, making it impossible for the eviction policy to differentiate between critical and expendable keys.

## Action items  
- **TTL enforcement:** Every key written to the cart cache must have a TTL. The `volatile-lru` policy now only evicts keys that have a TTL (completed in CHG‑2201).  
- **Memory alerting:** Implement an alert when `used_memory` exceeds 85 % of `maxmemory` (completed).

## Lessons learned  
- **TTL discipline is essential** for caches that store both transient and persistent data; without it, eviction policies cannot protect critical data.  
- **Memory capacity planning** must account for traffic spikes, especially during sales events, to avoid unintentional evictions.  
- **Proactive monitoring** of memory usage and eviction metrics can surface capacity issues before they impact customers.  
- **Separation of concerns**—storing different data lifecycles in separate Redis instances or using distinct key namespaces—reduces the risk of cross‑contamination during evictions.
