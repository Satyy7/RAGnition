# PM-2026-010: Shipping labels failing during back-to-school sale  
**Date:** 2026-09-03 | **Severity:** SEV2 | **Service:** shipping-api  

## Summary  
During the back‑to‑school sale, the shipping‑api service experienced a surge in parallel label and quote requests. The combined traffic exceeded the shared CarrierHub account limit of 50 requests/s, causing CarrierHub to return HTTP 429 responses. Shipping‑api propagated these as HTTP 503 errors to order‑worker, resulting in a backlog of label generation and slowed checkout quote calculations.

## Impact  
- Approximately 4,000 orders experienced delays of up to 70 minutes while waiting for a shipping label.  
- Checkout delivery quote calculations were noticeably slower, affecting the shopper experience.  

## Detection  
The incident was first detected by the **LabelFailureRate** alert, which fired at 09:14.

## Timeline  
- **09:00** – Sale begins; order‑worker scales out to handle increased traffic.  
- **09:14** – `LabelFailureRate` alert fires, indicating rising label generation failures.  
- **09:40** – Shipping‑api logs show CarrierHub responding with HTTP 429 (rate‑limit) errors.  
- **10:10** – Concurrency of order‑worker reduced; label backlog begins to drain and service recovers.  

## Root cause  
The combined volume of label and quote requests from order‑worker exceeded the shared CarrierHub account limit of **50 requests/s**. CarrierHub throttled the traffic with HTTP 429 responses, which shipping‑api translated into HTTP 503 errors for order‑worker.

## Trigger  
The scaling out of order‑worker for the sale caused a large number of parallel label requests, which together with quote requests pushed the total request rate over the CarrierHub limit.

## Resolution  
- Reduced order‑worker concurrency at 10:10, allowing the request rate to fall below the CarrierHub limit.  
- The reduced load let the label backlog drain, restoring normal label generation and quote performance.

## Contributing factors  
- No client‑side rate limiter in shipping‑api for the CarrierHub account.  
- Quotes and labels share a single CarrierHub account limit, amplifying the effect of traffic spikes.

## Action items  
- Any new CarrierHub traffic needs a capacity check against the 50 requests/s account limit (done, review checklist).  
- Implement a client‑side rate limiter in shipping‑api (open, **SHIP-52**).

## Lessons learned  
- **Capacity awareness is essential**: Services that share external API limits must verify that combined traffic stays within those limits, especially during anticipated load spikes.  
- **Rate limiting at the client**: Implementing client‑side throttling can prevent external services from returning rate‑limit errors and protect downstream services from propagating failures.  
- **Shared limits require coordinated monitoring**: When multiple features (e.g., label generation and quote calculation) use the same external account, their combined impact must be considered in alerting and scaling policies.  
- **Scaling decisions must account for downstream constraints**: Autoscaling of order‑worker increased request concurrency without regard for CarrierHub’s rate limits, highlighting the need for scaling rules that incorporate external dependency capacities.
