# PM-2026-011: Storefront features reverted to defaults during flag service outage  
**Date:** 2026-10-07 | **Severity:** SEV3 | **Service:** feature-flags  

## Summary  
During a planned failover of the flags database, the feature‑flags service was unavailable for 40 minutes. Services that started while the flag service was down fell back to their compiled default flag values, which were stale. As a result, some customers saw the old checkout page and legacy search ranking.

## Impact  
- Pods that restarted during the outage used compiled defaults instead of the current flag configuration.  
- Customers were served an outdated checkout page and legacy search ranking.  
- The issue was observed through customer reports and a measurable drop in search click‑through rates.

## Detection  
- Direct customer reports of the old checkout experience.  
- Monitoring showed a drop in search click‑through, indicating degraded search ranking.

## Timeline  
- **14:02** – Flags‑db failover starts.  
- **14:10** – First customer reports of the old checkout page.  
- **14:30** – Logs from services show “flag fetch failed, using defaults”.  
- **14:42** – Flag service becomes available again; affected pods are restarted to reload current flags.

## Root cause  
Clients that started while the flag service was unreachable fell back to the defaults compiled into each service. Those compiled defaults were not up‑to‑date with the production flag values, causing outdated behavior to be served.

## Trigger  
The failover of the `flags-db` made the feature‑flags service unavailable for 40 minutes.

## Resolution  
- The `flags-db` failover was completed, restoring the flag service.  
- Affected pods were restarted so they could fetch the current flag values from the restored service.

## Contributing factors  
- Compiled defaults were not kept in sync with the production flag values.

## Action items  
- **Alert when flag fetches fail in any client** – already implemented.  
- **Keep compiled defaults in sync with production values** – open ticket PLAT‑77.

## Lessons learned  
- Relying on compiled defaults during a flag service outage can introduce stale behavior if those defaults are not regularly synchronized with production flag values.  
- Monitoring for flag fetch failures at the client level provides early visibility into service unavailability.  
- Failover procedures should consider the downstream impact on services that depend on real‑time flag retrieval, ensuring that fallback mechanisms do not expose outdated functionality.
