# PM-2025-015: All payments failing - Paygate client certificate expired  
**Date:** 2025-07-21 **Severity:** SEV1 **Service:** payments‑api  

## Summary  
All payment authorizations failed for 64 minutes because the client certificate used for mutual TLS with the Paygate payment service provider had expired. The expired certificate caused TLS handshakes to be rejected, resulting in a 100 % error rate for payment requests.

## Impact  
- 100 % of payment authorizations failed.  
- The outage lasted 64 minutes, affecting all customers attempting to make a purchase during that window.

## Detection  
At 00:01 UTC the `PaymentsErrorRateHigh` alert fired. Log entries at that time showed the error message `remote error: tls: expired certificate`, indicating a TLS handshake failure with Paygate.

## Timeline  
- **00:00 UTC** – Paygate client certificate expired.  
- **00:01 UTC** – `PaymentsErrorRateHigh` alert triggered; logs showed TLS expiration error.  
- **00:40 UTC** – New client certificate issued via Paygate portal.  
- **01:04 UTC** – Pods restarted with the renewed certificate; payment processing recovered.

## Root cause  
The Paygate client certificate was renewed manually once a year. The calendar reminder for renewal was sent to a former employee, so the renewal was not performed before the certificate’s expiration. Paygate rejected the TLS handshake because the presented certificate was expired.

## Trigger  
The expiration of the client certificate used for mutual TLS with Paygate at 00:00 UTC.

## Resolution  
An emergency renewal of the client certificate was performed through the Paygate portal. The new certificate was stored as a secret, and the affected pods were restarted, restoring normal payment processing.

## Contributing factors  
- Manual renewal process with a single owner, leading to a single point of failure.  
- No monitoring in place for the expiry of external certificates used by payments‑api.

## Action items  
- Move Paygate client certificate to `cert-manager` (completed, CHG‑2150).  
- Implement an alert 21 days before any certificate in payments‑api expires, including entries in the trust bundle (completed).  
- Review vendor notices for certificate and CA changes in the weekly Payments sync (completed).

## Lessons learned  
- Relying on a single individual for manual certificate renewal creates a risk of missed expirations; ownership should be distributed or automated.  
- External certificates must be monitored for expiry just like internal ones to provide early warning before service impact.  
- Automated certificate management (e.g., `cert-manager`) reduces operational toil and eliminates human error in renewal processes.  
- Alerting well in advance of certificate expiration gives sufficient time to remediate without emergency actions.  
- Regular review of vendor communications ensures that any changes to certificate or CA requirements are captured proactively.
